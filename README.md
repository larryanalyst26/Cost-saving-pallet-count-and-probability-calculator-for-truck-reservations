# Cost-saving-pallet-count-and-probability-calculator-for-truck-reservations
This is a program I created in my previous dispatching job to find the most cost efficient truck size reservations for each city we shipped to. I want to note that this was done entirely by my own accord and was not asked of me. I spent time on this that was not paid extra for, so there are probably aspects where I could have went more in depth and definitely would have had I been in a technical role, which this was not.

The technical problem I was solving
Our warehouse received packages daily and sent packages to the same group of cities each day. Packages were sorted into pallets to be shipped to each city. Pallets are large cardboard boxes that held anywhere from 400 to 800 packages each. Some pallets were relatively empty with perhaps 200 packages. Truck costs varied for each city. There was a 26 foot truck and a 53 foot truck. The 26 held 13 pallets easily, but could also hold 14 if loaded with the proper technique and if the pallets weren't all huge, in which case you could only fit 13 even with loading skill. The 53 maxed out at 30 pretty much every time. The truck costs however were not proportional to their capacity. The 53 had a significant discount for all cities. The cost difference and ratios between the sizes varied for each city. I will give an example that is a very reasonable scenario, being that the 26 costed $600 and the 53 costed $750. However, 53's could only be reserved with at least a couple hours in advance. 26's could be added at any time, even during shipping time. Also, we were not to cancel trucks within 2 hours of shipping time. Shipping time begins with all pallets to be shipped are constructed. So in other words, we do not know how many pallets are to be shipped to each city by the time we must finalize truck reservations. We had to go off how many total packages for all cities combined there were. We did not know how many packages each city had by the time trucks had to be finalized. Before I wrote this function and started using it to reserve trucks, the other dispatchers were just eyeballing it and had to always be extra safe and get more trucks or bigger trucks than they needed, because it was absolutely not allowed to ever not ship all pallets each night. There were about 10 cities that we shipped to, and although you could have a general idea of how many pallets there would be with the total warehouse package count. Eyeballing it mad it impossible to save money in the scenarios, which happen everyday, where for example a city would probably have somewhere between 38 and 46 pallets. By eye, you can't just decide to reserve a 53 and a 26, because you don't know with precision that it would be 44 pallets or less. If it went over, you could only add another 26 and have 2 26's and a 53, which is war less efficient than 2 53's. So there policy before I wrote this function was to rather be wrong by getting more than they needed because it wasted less money to get 2 53's when they could have gotten a 53 and a 26 ($150 to be exact in the scenario I gave) than was wasted by trying to get a 53 and a 26 to save and then be wrong and have to add another 26 at shipping time when you could have just played safe and gotten 2 53's to start. The basic game we are trying to win at here is trying to take advantage of the $150 discount by getting a 53 and a 26 (versus 2 53's) and being able to fit the packages on them (with only the total combined packages to be shipped for all 10 cities) and doing this successfully enough times than we profit when subtracting the times we are wrong and have to add a 26 at the last moment and basically have. 53 and 2 26's, which in the pricing scenario I gave costs $450 than just playing it safe and getting 2 53's. So we need to be right, in this scenario, by more than 3 to 1 when compared to how often we are wrong, to add value. What my function does, is zero in on the true probability that in this scenario that particular city would have less than or equal to 43 pallets (be able to fit on a 53 and a 26).





The function takes 5 inputs. wtsl is a list of weights to use in the weighted linear regression for each day of data, from the most recent date to the least recent. xl is the list of independent inputs, which is the previous days' total warehouse package count, from most recent to least recent.
